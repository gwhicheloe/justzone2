How hard, and how often?

Intervals work, but more isn't better. We went through the research on how many hard sessions a week amateurs should do, and the answer is clearer than you might expect:

Twohard sessions a week is the right default for getting fitter, on anything from about four to fifteen hours.
48 h+between genuinely hard sessions. That's how long the heart's autonomic recovery takes after high-intensity work.
Easyeverything else. The days between need to be genuinely easy for the hard days to be hard.

One hard session a week is enough to start with, or to hold fitness through a busy patch. Three belongs in a short, planned block, not every week.

What is weighted average power?

Plain average power undersells interval sessions, because hard efforts cost disproportionately more than easy riding. Take the default session above (5 × 4 minutes at 220 W) and a session of ten 30-second efforts at 350 W, both with the same warm-up and cool-down. Their average powers are almost the same, 163 W and 167 W. Their weighted average powers are 182 W and 212 W, and the second one is the session that will hurt.

Weighted average power accounts for that. It takes the session second by second, smooths it with a 30-second rolling average, raises each value to the fourth power, averages those, and takes the fourth root. The result is closer to the steady power that would feel as hard. Compare two sessions by it to see which will hurt more.

Ride it in JustZone2

  1. In the app (version 1.0.2 or later), go to Settings and turn on Interval Builder.
  2. On the Workout tab, switch to Intervals and build your session. The controls are the same as above.
  3. Tap Start Intervals. Your trainer holds each interval's power in ERG mode, with a countdown to every interval and the session's shape on screen.

Interval rides upload to Strava under their own name and are kept out of your Zone 2 progress charts, so a hard day never muddies your aerobic trend.
