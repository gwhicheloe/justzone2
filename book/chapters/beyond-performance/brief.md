## The question

From the outline: does Zone 2 make you live longer, or is that a correlation? And, from the outline page, what easy riding does for health and weight. George's own question from Chapter 7, whether easy and hard training differ in their effect on colds, is answered here as far as the evidence allows.

## The argument (to approve)

1. **The position, stated on the first page:** fit and active people live longer, but how much of that is training and how much is the kind of person who becomes fit is not known; the one long randomised trial found no difference in deaths; training does improve blood pressure and blood sugar; most of the lower risk in the large surveys comes with the first few hours a week. A rider on six to ten hours is past that point, so the split between Zone 2 and intervals is a performance question, and the health evidence gives no reason to favour Zone 2. **My reading; grade C overall.**
2. Fit people live longer: Cleveland Clinic treadmill cohort (Mandsager 2018, read in full), with its limits; the dose curve from six pooled cohorts (Arem 2015), with my arithmetic placing a trained rider at roughly 7 to 13 times the guideline minimum; accelerometer data showing light activity counts (Ekelund 2019); cycle commuting (Celis-Morales 2017).
3. Reading the research, *marker or cause*: the London busmen as the origin of the question; illness, habit and inheritance; the Finnish twins, where identical twins discordant for exercise showed no difference in deaths (34 pairs; wide interval).
4. One study, up close: Generation 100 (Stensvold 2020, read in full), laid out as a three-group trial table. No difference in deaths against the guidelines; a non-significant trend favouring intervals over moderate training; controls very active; half adhered. Grade B for "five years of supervised training added nothing measurable to survival in healthy, active 70-year-olds".
5. Risk factors: blood pressure and HbA1c fall with training (grade A); in patients, intervals raised fitness and lowered insulin resistance more than continuous training (grade B).
6. Zone 2 as medicine, weighed: the observation is cross-sectional (San-Millán & Brooks 2018); the mitochondrial claim fails per hour (Chapter 7; Storoschuk 2025); the conclusion is contradicted by the trials. Steady exercise is good medicine, not special medicine (grade C).
7. Lifelong athletes: Tour de France riders live longer (Marijon 2013); more atrial fibrillation in frequent Vasaloppet skiers (Andersen 2013); more but more calcified coronary plaque in the most active men (Aengevaeren 2017). The balance looks favourable (grade C).
8. Colds: regular exercise goes with fewer (Nieman 2011); the marathon studies and the open window are disputed (Nieman 1990; Campbell & Turner 2018; Simpson 2020). Nothing compares easy with hard riding. George's winter fits, and so does riding more often (grade C).
9. Weight: exercise alone takes off a few kilograms over months, in proportion to the work (Donnelly 2013; Jelleyman 2015), whatever the intensity (grade B).
10. For six to ten hours: health is mostly banked; the best exercise for health is the one a person will keep doing.

## Weak points

- **Most sources are abstract only.** Read in full: Generation 100, Mandsager, the twin results of Karvinen, the relevant section of Campbell & Turner; Arem's results section from the PMC page.
- **The placing of a rider on the dose curve is my arithmetic**, from one example rider (VO₂max 55, riding at 60% of it) and the 3.5 mL/kg/min definition of a MET. The surveys assigned MET values to self-reported activities; they did not measure anyone riding.
- **The 1956 uniforms paper has not been read.** The draft cites it only for what its title says (busmen's physique studied from uniform sizes). I believe it found drivers were already bigger when they joined, and the draft does not say so until that is checked. The 1953 busmen paper is described from a 2001 history, not read.
- **The immune evidence is one side of a dispute**, balanced only by the abstract of the 2020 debate article.
- **The twin result is 34 identical pairs**, with an interval from half to twice the risk. The draft says this.
- **Generation 100's "moderate" group is not Zone 2 as this book defines it**; whether it was below each person's first threshold was not measured. The draft says this.
- **Short of target** by about 900 words. Candidates if George wants more: a section on fitness and the brain or mood; the accelerometer replication of the busmen study; more on which risk factors respond to intensity.
