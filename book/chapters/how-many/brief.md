## The question

Is two hard sessions a week really the right number? And Chapter 8's open question: is the champions' steady 1.3 hours a ceiling, and where is it for a rider on six to ten hours?

## The argument (to approve)

1. **The position:** two hard sessions a week, at least 48 hours apart, is the best-supported default; more only as short blocks; one maintains for months; spacing and easy days matter as much as the count; the hard dose that works in trials (40 to 65 minutes a week for cyclists) is close to the champions' dose, which suggests recovery sets the ceiling. Grade B for two; C for the ceiling.
2. Where two comes from: description, not test (Seiler & Kjerland 2006; Seiler 2010; Tønnessen 2014; the Norwegian rowers, Fiskerstrand 2004, whose hard training fell as they improved).
3. Adding hard sessions to a base: Lindsay 1996, Stepto 1999, Driller 2009.
4. Trials of frequency: Billat 1999 (one vs three; noradrenaline); Lenk 2025 (one, two, three); Helgerud 2007.
5. **Close-up: Tønnessen 2020**, as a table: two long threshold sessions against four short ones in elite skiers; only the two-session group improved significantly, **but the between-group effect for the time trial was trivial (0.17)**.
6. **Reading the research: a difference in significance.** Tønnessen 2020, Lenk 2025 (p = 0.17 for the group comparison) and Yang 2025 (0.69 vs 1.21) all show it. Compare groups with each other, not each with itself.
7. Recovery sets the ceiling: Stanley 2013 (24, 24 to 48, 48+ hours), Seiler 2007, the seven-day arithmetic, overreaching (Meeusen 2013; Halson & Jeukendrup 2004).
8. Arranging the work: block (Rønnestad 2014b; Breil 2010), compression (Hatle 2014), order (Sylta 2016). Grade B.
9. Threshold sessions count differently (the Norwegian model; Casado 2022 and 2023); my advice to count a controlled threshold session as a cheaper hard session. Grade C.
10. Maintenance: Hickson 1981 and 1985; Spiering 2021; masters (Borges 2016). Grade B.
11. Is the champions' hour a ceiling? My arithmetic across the trials. Grade C.
12. For six to ten hours: two, 48 to 72 hours apart; one when busy, new or flat; three only as a block.

## Corrections to the website article (for George)
- The website's hard-sessions article describes Tønnessen 2020 without saying the sessions were threshold intervals (82% to 87% of HRmax), and says "Only the two-session group improved its time-trial performance" without the trivial between-group effect size (0.17). The book says both. Worth correcting on the site.

## Weak points
- Every frequency trial is small (8 to 26) and short (4 to 12 weeks); none in time-crunched amateur cyclists over a season.
- Tønnessen 2020 is elite skiers doing threshold work; Lenk and Hatle are young recreational runners; Billat is runners.
- The dose comparison with the champions is my arithmetic across different ways of counting hard time.
- Helgerud 2007's men: whether they did anything besides the intervals is not in the abstract; the draft does not say.
