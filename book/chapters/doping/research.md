# Drugs: research notes

5 October 2026. **[FT]** full text read, **[Abs]** abstract, **[Rep]** news or encyclopaedia summary read through a tool, **[GK]** general knowledge not checked today: every [GK] and [Rep] item must be verified against a primary source before publication.

## Official record

- **CIRC report** (Cycling Independent Reform Commission; Dick Marty, Ulrich Haas, Peter Nicholson; dated Lausanne, February 2015) [FT, extracted from the PDF now in `papers/circ2015.pdf`; page numbers are the report's printed numbers]. `@circ2015`
  - 174 interviewees.
  - p. 34: "The media speculated that the deaths of 18 European cyclists in the late 1980s were related to EPO"; other estimates about 20 between 1987 and 1990; accounts of riders getting up at night to ride a stationary bike to keep blood circulating.
  - pp. 35–36: UCI "No Start Rule", 1997: haematocrit above 50% (men) or 47% (women) meant 15 days out; "not an anti-doping rule, but a health and safety measure". A former rider: it was "perceived by riders as legalising EPO up to a certain limit". Riders monitored themselves with centrifuges; a natural level of 43 to 45%; saline could dilute blood. Commission: the rule "probably prevented EPO-related deaths".
  - General finding: situation at elite level improved, doping still taking place, "a culture of doping in cycling continues to exist"; biggest concern micro-dosing under the biological passport; no testing between 11 pm and 6 am.
  - "The Commission was told that today doping performance gains are perhaps around only 3–5%, compared to 10–15% ten years ago." (hearsay; repeated p. 56–57)
  - p. 56: "One respected cycling professional felt that even today, 90% of the peloton was doping... Another put it at around 20%." Many said they didn't know.
  - p. 57: two core aims of doping in cycling: raising the blood's oxygen-carrying capacity; stimulating muscle growth and recovery.
  - Corticoids used to reduce pain and to lose weight; one doctor's claim of a 7% power-to-weight gain (hearsay). Tramadol "used widely" because strong and not banned (hearsay).
  - pp. 68–69: "doping in amateur cycling is becoming endemic"; "Masters races were also said to have middle-aged businessmen winning on EPO"; some professionals avoided Gran Fondos for that reason.
  - Historical: 1965 tests on Belgian cyclists found 37% of professionals and 23% of amateurs using amphetamines.
- **USADA Reasoned Decision**, 10 October 2012 [Rep, Wikipedia summary]: EPO, steroids, blood transfusions from 1996; 11 former teammates testified; lifetime ban; results from 1 August 1998 stripped, including seven Tours; UCI accepted 22 October 2012; no winners named for 1999 to 2005; Armstrong admitted doping in a television interview in January 2013. `@usada2012`
- **Festina affair** [Rep, Wikipedia]: Willy Voet stopped at the French-Belgian border 8 July 1998 with EPO, steroids and other products; team expelled 18 July; several Spanish teams withdrew; fewer than 100 of 189 starters finished; in court in October 2000 Richard Virenque admitted doping; Voet and team director Bruno Roussel received suspended sentences 22 December 2000. `@festina`
- **Operation Aderlass** [Rep, Wikipedia]: began February 2019 with the admissions of skier Johannes Dürr; police raids 27 February 2019; Erfurt doctor Mark Schmidt sentenced to 4 years 10 months on 16 January 2021; cyclists Stefan Denifl and Georg Preidler confessed in March 2019 (four-year bans); 21 athletes under suspicion. Uncovered by police, not by a positive test. `@aderlass`
- [GK, verify]: Pantani won the 1998 Tour; excluded from the 1999 Giro at Madonna di Campiglio with a haematocrit of 52%; died 2004. Tom Simpson died on Mont Ventoux, 13 July 1967, amphetamines found. Riccardo Riccò hospitalised February 2011 after a self-administered transfusion, later banned for 12 years. Recombinant EPO approved for kidney-disease anaemia in 1989. Urine test for EPO introduced 2000 (Lasne & de Ceaurriz, Nature 405:635: title confirmed, no abstract). Oxygen sensing by HIF won the 2019 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine. **None of these is in the draft unless marked.** Used in draft: Pantani 1998 win; Madonna di Campiglio 1999; EPO 1989; urine test 2000.

## Firmed up, 5 October 2026 (second pass)

- **López 2011** now **read in full** [FT] through the publisher's site in the browser (Sport in History 31(1):84–109). 61 texts reviewed (36 academic, 24 news reports, Kimmage's book); only two cite a more or less solid original source (a Dutch federation spokesperson, a Belgian sports physician), both in newspapers. Reported numbers of dead range from "half a dozen" to "around 40"; one academic source dates them 1997 to 2000. His own archive search: **49** sudden deaths of competitive cyclists 1987 to early 2010; **17** in 1987 to 1992 (9 Dutch, 6 Belgian, 1 British, 1 French); "an average of 1.25 sudden deaths per year per country". After removing riders long retired, off-season deaths and seven amateurs, **2** potentially suspicious cases remain. Johannes Draaijer died in his sleep in January 1990; his wife said post-mortem investigations showed nothing relating his death to EPO. Bille et al. (review): underlying cardiac disease accounts for about 90% of exercise-related sudden deaths. Besarab et al.: 1,233 haemodialysis patients with heart disease; the group held at a haematocrit of 42% had more heart attacks than the group at 30% (as reported by López; **Besarab not read**). López's own caveats: "not watertight scholarship"; no "absolute proof that EPO did not contribute". His stance is polemical ("anti-doping propaganda"). `@lopez2011`
- **1998 Tour** [Rep, Wikipedia "1998 Tour de France"]: started 11 July in Dublin; 189 starters, **96 finishers**; Festina expelled after stage 6 (17 July; the Festina-affair page says 18 July, so the draft gives the stage, not the date); ONCE, Banesto, Riso Scotti, Kelme, Vitalicio Seguros and TVM left later; Plateau de Beille was stage 11, 22 July, won by Pantani. French Senate report 2013: of 60 samples retested in 2004, **44 positive for EPO**. `@tour1998`
- **Pantani** [Rep, Wikipedia]: 1999 Giro, Madonna di Campiglio, haematocrit **52%** against the 50% threshold; expelled and stood down for two weeks; leading by 5:38; the page states he was never found to have used doping or disqualified for it; the Senate report listed him among riders whose retested 1998 samples were positive, with the caveat that the retests were not valid for sanctions; died 14 February 2004. The draft uses the 52%, the lead and "never sanctioned for doping" only. `@pantani`
- **EPO approval**: Epogen (epoetin alfa), FDA approval 1 June 1989 for anaemia of chronic renal failure; label reads "Initial U.S. Approval: 1989" [Rep: DailyMed label and trade press, via search results]. `@epogenlabel`
- **Heuberger 2017**: still abstract only. The Leiden repository holds a record but no file; the journal is paywalled. Added to `papers-wanted.md`. The draft's box uses only abstract figures.
- Still [Rep] and unverified at primary source: Festina court outcomes; Aderlass sentences and bans; USADA details (the Reasoned Decision itself is public and should be cited directly); UCI carbon monoxide and ketone statements (read through a tool); tramadol dates (UKAD page blocked the tool); Plateau de Beille times (unofficial).

## Festina court outcome, re-checked 6 October 2026

Three sources now agree on the verdict of the Tribunal correctionnel de Lille, **22 December 2000**: swissinfo (contemporary report, "Richard Virenque relaxé"), cyclisme-dopage.com ("Le jugement du procès Festina", a specialist site summarising the judgment and citing Libération of 23 December 2000) and Wikipedia (English and French). All [Rep], read through a summarising tool; **the judgment itself has not been read.**

- Team director (Bruno Roussel): **one year suspended**, fine 50,000 French francs.
- Soigneur (Willy Voet): **10 months suspended**, fine 30,000 francs.
- Others: a second soigneur 9 months suspended and 20,000 francs; two team staff 5 months suspended each; three fined (a doctor's fine was overturned on appeal, 5 March 2002). The team doctor's case was separated because of his health.
- The one rider tried (Richard Virenque, charged with complicity in incitement to doping) was **acquitted**; the prosecution had asked for no penalty. He admitted doping during the trial (24 October 2000 per Wikipedia). A sporting ban followed from the Swiss federation (9 months per Wikipedia; swissinfo says only that a decision was due on 29 December 2000). **Not in the draft: no rider is named or described.**
- Civil parties: at first instance the court **refused damages** to the sponsor, the UCI and the French federation, citing their "attitude ambiguë" in the fight against doping; on appeal in 2002 the two federations were awarded a symbolic one franc (cyclisme-dopage.com and French Wikipedia agree; wording of the judgment not seen).
- Discrepancy: swissinfo gives the fines as 12,000 and 8,000 francs (probably Swiss francs). The draft quotes no fines.
- Draft now says: director one year suspended, soigneur 10 months suspended, shorter suspended sentences and fines for five others, damages refused to governing bodies "according to reports of the judgment". "Five others" = the second soigneur, two staff and two fined at first instance whose fines stood (the Paraniers); the doctor later acquitted is not counted. **If in doubt, change to "several others".**

## Heuberger 2017 read in full, 6 October 2026 (PDF supplied by George, in `papers/heuberger2017.pdf`)

[FT] throughout. Corrects and replaces the abstract-only notes below.

- **Who:** 48 men aged 18 to 50 (plus one reserve); inclusion: more than 4 W/kg in the screening ramp test, haemoglobin 8.0 to 9.8 mmol/L, haematocrit below 48%, **not subject to anti-doping regulation**. Enrolled March to April 2016. Baseline VO₂max **55.63 mL/kg/min**, maximal power **335 W** (5-minute steps, which read lower than short protocols). Training recorded during the study: **4.9 h/week (EPO) against 5.9 h/week (placebo)**; authors: "substantially less" than professionals (over 20 h).
- **Treatment:** eight weekly subcutaneous injections of epoetin beta; **5,000 IU for the first four**, then 6,000, 8,000 or 10,000 IU to reach a haemoglobin rise of 10 to 15%; placebo injection given instead if haematocrit reached **52%** (five occasions). Mean **6,000 IU/week**, 48,000 IU in total. Everyone took iron and vitamin C. Placebo visually identical. Authors: the dose "is also consistent with known practices in professional cycling"; earlier studies used 4,500 to 26,000 IU/week without a clear dose-response.
- **Blood:** haemoglobin **+12%** (to 10.2 mmol/L); haematocrit **+16% to 50%**; over the treatment period 47.6% against 44.3%. Still +12% at the pre-race measurement. 14 of 23 reached more than +10%.
- **Maximal test:** VO₂max about **5%** and maximal power about **4%** higher than placebo (351.6 against 341.2 W). Effects already visible at day 11, before haemoglobin had risen much (authors suggest other mechanisms).
- **45-minute test (day 46):** 283.2 against 277.3 W, difference **5.9 W (CI −0.9 to 12.7)**; authors call the study "adequately powered" for this. Our arithmetic: 5.9/277 = 2.1%; interval about −0.3% to +4.6%.
- **Mont Ventoux:** afternoon of **19 June 2016**, about **12 days (range 10 to 16)** after the last dose; preceded by a **110 km** stage (1,524 m of climbing) ridden as a closed pack; then Bédoin to the summit, **21.5 km, 1,610 m, 7.5%**. Weather: Bédoin about 20 °C, 40 km/h northerly; summit about **5 °C, 85 km/h** northerly. **44 started (21 EPO)**; **4 did not finish** (2 each, exhaustion). Times **1:40:32 against 1:40:15** (0.3%, CI −8.3 to 9.6); power **3.03 against 3.09 W/kg** (−1.7%, CI −11.0 to 8.6).
- **Guessing treatment:** 27 of 47 correct (57%). Of 23 on EPO, **9** thought so; of 24 on placebo, **6** thought they had had EPO.
- **Planning:** sample size set on VO₂max. Authors' own estimate of a meaningful effect: 0.172 W/kg on 5.2 W/kg in professionals (our arithmetic: 3.3%), "approximately 2 min on a climb like Mont Ventoux".
- **Safety:** no difference in adverse events, no clinical thrombosis, no effect on blood pressure or coagulation factors; **E-selectin and P-selectin rose** (involved in clot formation); stroke incidence in treated patients given as 2.6%; study not powered to detect such events; authors think risk in uncontrolled use "not unlikely" to be raised.
- **Authors' conclusion:** improved the maximal test; no improvement in the submaximal test or the road race.
- **An earlier draft error, now moot:** the first draft said the race was "twelve days after the last injection"; that was removed as unsourced and turns out to be what the paper says.
- **Our reasoning in the draft:** the comparison of the confidence intervals with the 3 to 5% and two-minute figures; "roughly eight minutes either way" (8.3% and 9.6% of 100 minutes); "a five-hour day" (110 km plus the climb: an estimate, soften if challenged); the Grand Tour comparison for 2% (illustrative, unsourced).

## Biology of EPO

- **Jelkmann 2011** (J Physiol 589:1251–1258) [Abs]: EPO maintains red cell mass by promoting survival, proliferation and differentiation of red cell precursors; made mainly by fibroblasts in the kidney cortex; production switched on by low oxygen through hypoxia-inducible factors (mainly HIF-2), which are destroyed when oxygen is plentiful; EPO deficiency is the main cause of anaemia in chronic kidney disease, treated with recombinant EPO. `@jelkmann2011`
- **Lundby et al. 2007** (J Physiol 578:309–314) [Abs]: 8 healthy people, EPO for 15 weeks aiming at a haematocrit of about 50%. Red cell volume **2,933 → about 3,200 mL**; plasma volume **3,645 → about 3,270 mL**; total blood volume essentially unchanged (6,578 → 6,477 mL at 5 weeks). Between **38% and 54%** of the rise in the blood's oxygen content came from the fall in plasma. `@lundby2007`

## How much it is worth

- **Thomsen et al. 2007** (Eur J Appl Physiol 101:481–486) [Abs]: 8 healthy non-athletes. VO₂max **+12.6%** at 4 weeks, +11.6% at 11; time to exhaustion at 80% **+54%**. `@thomsen2007`
- **Heuberger et al. 2017** (Lancet Haematol 4:e374–386; Leiden) [Abs]: double-blind, randomised, placebo-controlled; 48 well-trained amateur men aged 18 to 50; EPO (mean 6,000 IU a week) or saline for 8 weeks. Haemoglobin **9.6 against 9.0 mmol/L**. Maximal power **351.6 against 341.2 W** (difference 10.3, CI 3.5 to 17.2); VO₂max **60.1 against 57.4 mL/kg/min** (2.7, CI 0.9 to 4.5). 45-minute test power 283 against 277 W (5.9, CI −0.9 to 12.7: not significant). Mont Ventoux race **1:40:32 (EPO) against 1:40:15 (placebo)**: difference 0.3%, CI **−8.3% to +9.6%**. No serious adverse events. `@heuberger2017`
- **Alberdi-Garciandia & Santos-Concejero 2025** (Sports 13:78; open access) [Abs]: systematic review, 10 studies in well-trained athletes: haemoglobin and VO₂max rise whatever the dose; maximal power tends to rise; effect on submaximal performance unresolved. `@alberdi2025`
- **Bejder et al. 2019** (MSSE 51:692–700) [Abs]: double-blind crossover, highly trained men; re-infusing about 135 mL of their own red cells raised time-trial power **213 → 223 W (about 5%)**; no change after sham (n = 7). `@bejder2019`
- **Ashenden et al. 2011** (Eur J Appl Physiol 111:2307–2314) [Abs]: 10 people micro-dosed EPO for up to 12 weeks; haemoglobin mass **+10%**, "equivalent to approximately two bags of reinfused blood"; the passport software flagged none of them. `@ashenden2011`

## Race speeds and prevalence

- **Perneger 2010** (Int J Sports Med 31:261–264) [Abs]: speed of fifth-place finishers in the three Grand Tours, 1990 to 2009: rising 0.16 km/h a year to 2004, falling 0.22 km/h a year after. `@perneger2010`
- **Lodewijkx & Brouwer 2011** (Res Q Exerc Sport 82:740–754) [Abs]: winners' times in 181 Grand Tours 1947 to 2008; no sharp break; the effect of the EPO era "may be overestimated". `@lodewijkx2011`
- **de Hon et al. 2015** (Sports Med 45:57–69) [Abs]: across elite sport, best estimates of intentional doping **14 to 39%**, against **1 to 2%** of tests positive a year; performance data cannot give prevalence. `@dehon2015`
- **Zorzoli & Rossi 2010** (Drug Test Anal 2:542–547) [Abs]: UCI biological passport from January 2008; more than 850 riders; almost 20,000 samples in 2008 and 2009. `@zorzoli2010`
- **López 2011** (Sport in History; "The invention of a 'drug of mass destruction': deconstructing the EPO myth") [Rep: secondary summaries only; **paper not read**]: examined 36 academic texts repeating the EPO-deaths claim and found no evidence that EPO caused the deaths. Draft attributes this carefully; **read the paper before final.** `@lopez2011`

## Now

- **Plateau de Beille**: Pantani 1998, **43:28 to 43:30**; Pogačar, stage 15, 2024 Tour, **39:41 to 39:50** depending on the timer [Rep: Cyclingnews, Canadian Cycling Magazine, climbing-records.com]. Draft says "about three and a half minutes faster" and gives no power estimate. `@cyclingnews2024beille`
- **Pogačar's explanation** (L'Équipe interview reported by Canadian Cycling Magazine, 16 July 2024) [Rep]: teams focusing on "nutrition, technology, training plans, and altitude camps"; "Bicycles have evolved so much over the last five years" (tyres, wheels, aerodynamic frames). `@ccm2024`
- In this book: Norte 2026 (critical power after 3 h: 236 W on water, 266 W on 120 g/h); Cubel 2026 (altitude +3.5%, heat +5.4% haemoglobin mass).
- **Carbon monoxide**: UCI press release 1 February 2025 [Rep, read through a tool]: from 10 February 2025 bans possession, outside a medical facility, of commercial CO rebreathing systems; one inhalation to measure haemoglobin mass still allowed under medical supervision, a second only two weeks later; reason given: health; WADA asked to take a position. First reported by Escape Collective, July 2024 [Rep, via road.cc; article not read]. `@uci2025co`
- **Tramadol**: banned in competition by the UCI from 1 March 2019, by WADA from 1 January 2024 [Rep]. Holgado et al. 2018 (J Sci Med Sport 21:654–660) [Abs]: 20-minute time trial, **220 W tramadol against 209 W placebo** in the first experiment; no difference (234 against 230 W) in the second. `@holgado2018`
- **Ketones**: UCI statement 20 October 2025 recommending riders not use them, "no compelling evidence" of benefit; not banned [Rep]. `@uci2025ketones`
