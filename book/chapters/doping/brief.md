## Legal rule for this chapter (libel)

George will not be getting a lawyer's review (6 October 2026), so the chapter is written to carry as little risk as it can. Rules:

- A living person is connected with doping **only** on the strength of a published decision of an anti-doping body or a court **and** their own public admission. As drafted that is one person: Lance Armstrong (USADA Reasoned Decision, October 2012; televised admission, January 2013).
- **No current rider is named anywhere in the chapter**, for any reason. The original opening compared a 2024 climbing time with a 1998 record; George asked for it to be removed (6 October) and it must not come back in another form. No climb times, no power estimates, no "faster than the EPO era" comparisons tied to a person or a race.
- People convicted or sanctioned in the Festina and Aderlass cases are referred to by role (team director, soigneur, doctor, riders), not by name.
- The dead are named only where the record is clear and the statement is neutral or exculpatory: Marco Pantani (1999 haematocrit reading under a health rule; "not a doping sanction"). The Dutch rider in the López paper is not named.
- Retrospective test results that were never valid for sanctions (the French Senate list of 1998 samples) are **not used at all**.
- Estimates from interviewees in the CIRC report are quoted as what one person told the commission, about a class of people, never about an individual or team.
- No team is named in connection with carbon monoxide, ketones, tramadol or exemptions. Festina is named as the subject of a court case.
- The chapter states in its opening that nobody is connected with doping unless officially found or self-admitted, and that George assumes today's riders are clean.
- Opinions are marked as George's opinions.

**People named in the draft and why (check this list whenever the chapter is edited):** Lance Armstrong (as above); Marco Pantani (deceased; as above); Bernat López and Carsten Lundby (researchers, for their published work). Nobody else.

This is care, not legal advice. If the book sells widely, a paid review of this one chapter is still the safer course.

## The argument

1. EPO-era doping was aimed at one link in the chain from Chapter 1: the oxygen the blood can carry. The biology is well understood, and the laboratory effect is large and certain.
2. How much it was worth on the road is less certain than its reputation. The only randomised trial with a road race found no effect in amateurs, with a confidence interval too wide to rule out the 3 to 5% that matters. The evidence for professionals is indirect: confessions, race speeds, the commission's testimony.
3. The risks are partly myth. The famous "18 dead cyclists" has no evidence behind it. The documented harms are individual medical emergencies, bans, convictions, erased results and a sport nobody could believe.
4. Lessons: tests miss most doping; rules shape behaviour (the 50% rule); a performance cannot prove either guilt or innocence; and doping has moved into amateur and masters racing.
5. How riders improve without drugs. Legal explanations each have evidence, some of it in this book: fuelling, altitude and heat, equipment, training precision. None of that can prove anyone clean, and the chapter says nothing can.
6. The boundary keeps moving: tramadol, carbon monoxide, ketones. George's position, stated as his: he assumes they are clean and thinks the edges are being pushed.
