## The question
What changes for masters riders, women, and riders who are short of time or training indoors?

## The argument (to approve)
1. **The position:** the rules carry across; what changes is degree, and the research is thinner for each group. Grade C overall.
2. Older riders: Pollock 1997 (opening and figure): every group lost VO₂max over 20 years, the hardest-training least; maximum heart rate falls 5–7 bpm a decade, so zones set from an old maximum are too high. Tanaka & Seals 2008: VO₂max drives the decline, economy holds, and much of the decline comes from training less hard. Recovery: Fell & Williams 2008 and Borges 2016 (less certain than believed). Lifting for bone. Intervals are not unsafe because of age (Generation 100; Wisløff 2007), with the medical caveat.
3. Women: *Reading the research: who was in the study?* (Ose 2025, figure). Trainability (Diaz-Canestro & Montero 2019: men gained more VO₂max, untrained, eight studies); Helgerud 2023 and Urianstad 2024 in trained women. Fatigability (Hunter 2014; Smyth 2022). Menstrual cycle (McNulty 2020: trivial on average; personal approach; the cycle-syncing fashion). Contraception (Elliott-Sale 2020). Energy availability (Fuelling). Pregnancy and menopause: out of scope, said so.
4. Indoors: *One study, up close*: Mieras 2014 as a trial table (about a fifth less power at the same effort). Heat and still air (Saunders 2005; cardiovascular drift). ERG mode: hold power or hold heart rate (no study found).
5. Short of time: pointers to The training week, How many and Designing intervals; regularity.

## Weak points
- About 4,000 words short. Candidates: training for masters riders in more detail (protein and recovery; masters cyclists' training studies), iron and women riders, young riders, and riders returning after a long break.
- Almost everything is abstract only. Ose 2025's 40 million participants are dominated by a few large datasets (my inference); the per-study figures are used.
- The trainability meta-analysis contradicts the book's earlier tone that women respond as men do; the draft reports it plainly and the Designing intervals chapter's "the sessions work the same way in women" should be read against it.
- Mieras 2014 doesn't say what the riders rode indoors.
