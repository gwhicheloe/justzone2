## The question

What do "Zone 2", "threshold" and "easy" mean, and what in the body do they refer to?

## The argument (to approve)

1. "Zone 2" names two different intensities. In the three-zone model of most research it is the band between the thresholds; in five-zone models it is the top of the easy range. Seiler's 2006 and 2025 papers show both uses.
2. The body has two boundaries and three domains, and they are real: Black 2017 rode people to exhaustion in each (3.5 hours, 43 minutes, 2 to 14 minutes) and found different reasons for stopping (grade B).
3. Lactate is a fuel in transit and its blood level is a balance. The "anaerobic" explanation is wrong and the name survives. Breathing changes because of buffering; oxygen cost creeps above the first threshold.
4. The first threshold: lactate and breathing methods agree roughly; it sits at 60 to 90% of maximum heart rate depending on the person (Iannetta, grade B); it moves with training and within a ride.
5. The second threshold is disputed: 4 mmol/L, MLSS, critical power and FTP are different things. The 4 mmol rule overshoots by 14 to 41 W in one study; critical power has the best case as the true boundary, argued by its own proponents.
6. FTP agrees with MLSS on average in one study and not in another; for an individual it can be 9% out either way.
7. Zone systems anchored to one number (Coggan's) assume a fixed gap between thresholds. Counting by session or by minutes changes "80/20" to "96/4".
8. The 2025 consensus (grade C) defines Zone 2 as immediately below the first threshold; the book adopts it. Common markers of Zone 2 span 125 to 224 W in the same riders (Meixner, grade B).
9. Power, heart rate and feel measure different things and come apart over a long ride.
10. A fixed vocabulary for the rest of the book.

## Changed 11 October, after George's comment on Finding your zones

George asked for the book to be more opinionated about what the zones are. The section *What Zone 2 means now* gained a paragraph in which the book chooses between the panel's two options: **the top of Zone 2 is the first lactate threshold, the last intensity before lactate rises 0.5 mmol/L above the rider's own baseline, in a test with stages of at least four minutes.** The first entry in the closing list of terms was changed to match. The reasons, and the evidence that the choice is a practical one and not a proven one, are in Finding your zones. The exact rule (0.5 above baseline, four-minute stages) is my proposal and awaits George's confirmation.
