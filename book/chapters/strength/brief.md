## The question

Does lifting weights make a trained cyclist faster, how, and is there any other reason to do it?

## The argument

1. The fear that lifting and endurance training don't mix comes from Hickson 1980, which showed endurance training blunting strength gains, not the reverse. Hickson 1988 showed trained cyclists lasting longer after adding leg weights.
2. Trials in trained cyclists (mostly Scandinavian, 12 to 25 weeks, heavy leg exercises twice a week) mostly find better performance in efforts of 5 to 45 minutes; one well-run trial in women (Bishop 1999) found nothing.
3. A 2026 meta-analysis of 17 studies finds a moderate effect on performance and none on VO₂max or threshold; the gain comes through efficiency and force production. Certainty low; most studies unrandomised; four of nine performance studies from one group (grade B).
4. Proposed mechanisms: slow-twitch fibres doing more of the work, IIX to IIA fibre shift, faster force development. Correlational.
5. Cyclists on a full endurance programme gain strength with little or no weight.
6. Twice a week for about 12 weeks builds it; once a week maintains it.
7. Road cyclists have low bone density (C, observational). That is a reason to lift that has nothing to do with speed, though no trial shows lifting fixes it in cyclists.
8. It is not new and nobody has tested whether it explains faster racing.
