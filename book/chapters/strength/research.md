# Strength training: research notes

Written 11 October 2026. Tags: [FT] full text; [Abs] abstract; [Rep] reported by a source I read; [GK] general knowledge, unchecked; [Own] my arithmetic.

**How far each source was read.** Rønnestad & Mujika 2014: accepted manuscript from George, cycling and practical sections. Llanos-Lagos 2026: full text for abstract, methods, main results, risk of bias, the performance discussion and the practical table; not every table. Everything else: abstract only. Olmedillas 2012 is open access and still to read.

## Hickson [Abs]

- 1980: strength group 30 to 40 min a day, 5 days a week; endurance group 40 min a day, 6 days a week; combined group did both. 10 weeks. VO₂max up about 25% (bicycle) and 20% (treadmill) in the endurance and combined groups, not in the strength group. Combined group's strength matched the strength group's for 7 weeks, "subsequently leveled off and declined during the 9th and 10th weeks". Group sizes not in the abstract.
- 1988: eight cycling- and running-trained subjects "already at a steady-state level of performance"; heavy resistance 3 days a week for 10 weeks, endurance training constant. Leg strength +30%; thigh girth, fibre areas, citrate synthase and VO₂max unchanged; short-term endurance (4 to 8 min) +11% cycling, +13% running; cycling to exhaustion at 80% VO₂max 71 to 85 min; 10 km running inconclusive. No control group.
- Hickson's first name (Robert), and "many endurance athletes stayed out of the gym": [GK].

## Trials in cyclists [all Abs]

- Rønnestad 2010a: 20 well-trained cyclists "assigned" to E+S (n = 11, all men) or E (n = 9; 7 men, 2 women); four lower-body exercises, 3 × 4 to 10 RM, twice a week, 12 weeks. E+S increased thigh CSA, isometric force and Wingate peak power more than E; power at 2 mmol/L lactate and 40-min power improved in E+S; E's 40-min power "tended to improve (P = 0.057)"; VO₂max rose similarly in both. CSA up "without causing an increase in body mass".
- Rønnestad 2011: identical group description. After 185 min at 44% of maximal aerobic power, 5-min power 371 ± 9 to 400 ± 13 W in E+S, no change in E. Also in the Durability notes.
- **The two abstracts describe the same group sizes, sexes and programme. I infer one cohort; neither abstract says so.** Check in the full texts before publication.
- Aagaard 2011: 14 young elite cyclists, 16 weeks. MVC and RFD +12 to 20% with strength, not without; VO₂max unchanged; 5-min +3 to 4% in both; 45-min +8% with strength only; IIA up, IIX down; fibre area and capillaries unchanged. Danish: [GK].
- Rønnestad 2015: 25 weeks; 9 ES, 7 E, young elite. ES superior in Wmax and 40-min power; lean lower-body mass increased; earlier peak torque (r = 0.66 with 40-min change); no change in VO₂max or economy.
- Vikmoen 2016: 19 well-trained women "randomly assigned"; E+S n = 11, E n = 8; 11 weeks. 40-min power, fractional utilisation, economy improved; IIAX-IIX to IIA; r = −0.63 (fibre shift) and 0.73 (CSA) with performance change; no changes in E.
- Bishop 1999: 21 endurance-trained women aged 18 to 42, randomly assigned (RT 14, control 7); parallel squats, five sets to failure at 2 to 8 RM, twice a week, 12 weeks. 1RM +35.9% vs +3.7%; no change in 1-h power, lactate threshold, VO₂, fibre characteristics or enzymes. Australian: [GK].
- Rønnestad 2010b: E+S n = 6, E n = 6. Twice a week in a 12-week preparatory period, once a week for 13 weeks of the competition period; CSA and strength preserved; larger relative gains in Wmax and 40-min power than E.
- Yamamoto 2010: RCTs only, to September 2009, highly trained road cyclists; five included; two found no improvement (strength added on top), three improved (replaced part of endurance training).
- **Rønnestad & Mujika 2014 (review): accepted manuscript supplied by George 11 October 2026 (`papers/ronnestad2014.pdf`). Read for the cycling material, mechanisms, potential negative outcomes, practical recommendations and conclusion; the running sections only skimmed.** [FT]
  - Heavy strength training defined as loads allowing 1 to 15 RM; explosive as 0 to 60% of 1RM moved as fast as possible.
  - VO₂max: "only a trivial effect" of added strength training in trained cyclists.
  - The positive effect on time to exhaustion at maximal power "was not observed by using explosive strength training (Bastiaans et al., 2001) nor after short-term (6 weeks) strength training (Levin et al. 2009)". It lists Bishop 1999 among studies with no effect on lactate-threshold power and offers no explanation for it.
  - Mechanism: with greater maximal force, the peak force of each movement cycle at a given intensity "decreases to a lower percentage of the maximal values"; a cross-sectional study (Bieuzen 2007) found lower EMG activity in stronger cyclists of similar VO₂max and Wmax.
  - "8-16 weeks of supplemental strength training failed to increase total body mass" in cyclists, runners, triathletes and skiers; "a small, ~3-6%, increase in measurements of muscle hypertrophy of the main target muscles".
  - Impaired hypertrophy: AMPK activated by endurance exercise "may inhibit mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR) signalling and suppress strength exercise-induced myofibrillar protein synthesis". The draft does not name mTOR.
  - No impairment of oxidative enzymes or capillarisation reported; "no negative effects of adding strength training to an endurance training program have been reported".
  - Practical: exercises should "involve similar muscle groups and imitate the sports specific movements"; intended, not actual, velocity determines the response (Heggelund 2013: 8 weeks, maximal against moderate concentric velocity, superior strength and RFD); two sessions a week for 12 weeks; 4RM to 10RM, 2 to 3 sets, 2 to 3 min rest; "first developed a proper lifting technique with lighter loads"; "heavy" and "sore" legs at the start, so "commence at low level with the concurrent endurance training during the first two to three weeks"; begin "just after the end of a competition season"; about one low-volume, high-intensity session a week maintains.
  - Rønnestad is first author of the review and led most of the Norwegian trials. From Lillehammer.
  - **The review does not say whether Rønnestad 2010a and 2011 used the same riders.** That inference still rests on the two abstracts.

## Llanos-Lagos 2026 [FT]

- Heavy = at least 80% of 1RM, at least 3 weeks. Randomised and non-randomised controlled studies. 17 studies, 262 participants (145 experimental, 117 control; 60 women); mean VO₂max 61.25; 5 to 25 weeks; 1 to 3 sessions a week.
- Effects (ES, 95% CI): VO₂max −0.041 (−0.345 to 0.263); pVO₂max 0.164 (−0.176 to 0.505); MMSS 0.069 (−0.323 to 0.460); anaerobic capacity 0.235 (−0.230 to 0.700); anaerobic power 0.560 (0.097 to 1.023); cycling efficiency 0.353 (interval printed as −0.619 to −0.088); performance 0.463 (0.109 to 0.817).
- Performance assessed in nine studies: Bishop, Sunde, Rønnestad (three), Aagaard, Vikmoen, Ji, Sitko. Four of nine from the Rønnestad group: [Own count; Vikmoen has Rønnestad as senior author].
- Risk of bias moderate, mean 5.53 of 7, "mainly due to a lack of group randomisation and/or blinded group allocation". No publication bias detected. Certainty low for every outcome.
- "The protocol was registered on the Open Science Framework after the data analysis."
- No moderating effect of sex, body mass, height, performance level, strength experience, duration, frequency or total sessions.
- Programme averages (Table 5): about 4 exercises (1 to 7); about 84% 1RM or 7RM; about 14 weeks. Exercise frequency across studies: squat 12, ankle plantar flexion 6, leg curls 5, leg press 4, leg extension 3.
- No conflicts of interest declared.
- An umbrella review (Ramos-Campo 2025; 17 reviews) reaches the same direction and rates most reviews as low or critically low confidence. [Abs; not cited in the draft]

## Bone [Abs]

- Nichols 2003: 27 master cyclists (51.2 years; racing 20.2 years; little weight-bearing exercise), 16 young adult cyclists, 24 non-athletes matched for age and weight ("normally active"). Spine and total hip BMD lower in master cyclists than both other groups; 4 (15%) with T-scores below −2.5, none in the other groups. California: [GK]. T-score of −2.5 as the osteoporosis threshold: [GK].
- Olmedillas 2012: 31 studies; adult road cyclists have low BMD in key regions such as the lumbar spine; mountain biking or combination with other sports "could reduce this unsafe effect"; road cycling "does not appear to confer any significant osteogenic benefit".
- That lifting restores bone in cyclists: **no source found; not claimed.** "Loading the skeleton is the standard advice": [GK].
