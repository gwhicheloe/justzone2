# How bodies adapt to training: research notes

Written 10 October 2026. Tags: [FT] read in the full text; [Abs] stated in the paper's abstract; [Rep] reported by another source; [GK] general knowledge, not checked against a source for this book.

## The Dallas Bed Rest and Training Study (McGuire 2001)

- Five healthy men first studied in 1966, retested 30 years later. [Abs]
- VO₂max 3.30 L/min in 1966 against 2.90 L/min 30 years on, a fall of 11%. [Abs]
- Body weight 77 kg then, 100 kg later [Abs, used]; body fat 14% then, 28% later [Abs, not used].
- Per kilogram of body mass: 43 then, 31 mL/kg/min later. [Abs] Added 10 Oct after George's comment that 2.90 did not look like a VO₂max: the draft now has a one-line note that the figures are whole-body totals; the weight and per-kilogram figures are not used (George asked for a quick note only).
- "3 weeks of bedrest in these same men at 20 years of age (1966) had a more profound impact on physical work capacity than did 3 decades of aging." [Abs]
- The men were then trained, in the same 1966 study. [GK: Saltin et al. 1968, not read. No numbers from it are used.]

## Signals

- Contraction raises ATP turnover, calcium flux and mechanical stress, which start signalling pathways that act on PGC-1α-dependent gene programmes. [Abs, Egan 2010]
- ATP (three phosphates; muscle stores only a few seconds' worth), ADP, AMP and AMPK as the cell's energy sensor; calcium release triggering contraction; CaMK. [GK: textbook biochemistry. Egan & Zierath 2013 is cited for the overall picture, abstract only.]
- DNA to messenger RNA to protein. [GK]

## PGC-1α

- Full name: peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma coactivator 1-alpha (PPARγ coactivator-1α). [GK; used in the draft's opening of "How it works" for a dry line, 15 October 2026]
- Found in 1998 in a brown-fat library from mice; its mRNA rises sharply in brown fat and skeletal muscle on cold exposure; put into white fat cells it switches on mitochondrial respiratory-chain enzymes and raises mitochondrial DNA. [Abs, Puigserver 1998]
- Mice engineered to make more PGC-1α in muscle: muscles normally rich in type II fibres become redder, switch on mitochondrial genes, make type I proteins and resist fatigue from electrical stimulation much longer. [Abs, Lin 2002]
- Mice with no PGC-1α increased mitochondrial enzymes with five weeks of training about as much as normal mice (Leick et al. 2008). [Rep, by Perry 2010, Discussion. Leick 2008 itself not read.] Perry's reading: PGC-1α probably mediates the response but other mechanisms exist.

## Perry 2010 (One study, up close) [all FT]

- Nine men, mean age 23, not in structured training; light aerobic activity about an hour a day on two to three days a week.
- Seven sessions over about two weeks, three days a week. Each: 10 × 4 minutes at about 90% of peak oxygen uptake, 2 minutes rest between.
- Biopsies of the thigh (vastus lateralis): one before, then 4 h and 24 h after sessions 1, 3, 5 and 7. Nine biopsies each, alternating legs.
- PGC-1α mRNA more than 10-fold at 4 h after session 1, back to the starting level by 24 h. Same pattern after each session, with a smaller rise each time.
- PGC-1α protein +23% 24 h after session 1; plateau of +42% by sessions 5 to 7 (Results; the abstract says +30 to 40%). Draft says "about 40%".
- Citrate synthase mRNA +48% after session 1; citrate synthase activity first up (+17%) after session 3. Three sessions took five days (Discussion).
- Mitochondrial DNA +65% 24 h after session 3.
- COX-IV protein first up (+20%) 24 h after session 5.
- Peak oxygen uptake +12% (3.74 to 4.18 L/min). Training power 203 W in session 1, 239 W in session 7 (+18%), raised to hold heart rate at about 183 bpm.
- The mRNA response shrank "despite a continual increase in training power output". Authors suggest this may explain plateaus (their speculation).
- Limits: no control group; no statistics beyond repeated-measures comparison with the first biopsy; n = 6 to 9 for some proteins; authors spend six paragraphs defending the PGC-1α antibody; intervals only, untrained men only, two weeks only.

## Muscle

- Holloszy 1967: rats trained on a treadmill; muscle mitochondrial enzyme capacity rose about twofold. **[GK, from memory. The paper has no abstract in Europe PMC and the JBC page did not load. Check before publication; on papers-wanted.]**
- Andersen & Henriksson 1977: five subjects, eight weeks, 40 minutes a day, four times a week, at 80% of VO₂max. VO₂max +16%, capillary density +20%, mean fibre area +20%, succinate dehydrogenase and cytochrome oxidase about +40%. [Abs]
- Montero 2015: six weeks: capillary-to-fibre ratio +18 ± 16%, total mitochondrial volume density +43 ± 30%; respiratory capacity (OXPHOS) unchanged. [Abs]
- Why more mitochondria lower lactate at a given power: Chapter 1, from Bassett & Howley 2000 (already in that chapter's notes). The "each mitochondrion works at a smaller fraction of its capacity" explanation is Holloszy's classic account. [GK]
- Bishop 2014 (review): training intensity may determine mitochondrial respiration but not content (citrate synthase); training volume may determine content. "Due to the small number of studies, more research is required to verify the trends." Adaptations "quickly reversed" on stopping. [Abs]
- Granata 2018 (review): same conclusion on volume and intensity; "exercise-induced changes are not necessarily predictive of training-induced adaptations". [Abs]
- MacInnis 2017: 10 active young men; one leg 4 × 5 min at 65% of single-leg peak power, the other 30 min at 50%; six sessions over two weeks; work matched (143 vs 144 kJ per session). Citrate synthase activity after training 10.2 in the interval leg against 8.4 in the continuous leg. [Abs] (10.2 / 8.4 = 1.21, "about a fifth higher": my arithmetic.)

## Blood and heart

- Convertino 1991 (review): plasma volume expansion accounts for nearly all the rise in blood volume up to two to four weeks; after that the expansion may be shared equally between plasma and red cells. Benefits: more fluid for heat loss, larger filling of the heart, greater stroke volume, lower heart rate. [Abs]
- Montero 2015: 16 untrained men (25 ± 4 years), six weeks, three to four supervised sessions a week. VO₂peak +9 ± 8%, peak cardiac output +7 ± 6%. Red cell volume +8 ± 4%, plasma volume +4 ± 6%, haemoglobin mass +6 ± 5%. Then red cell volume returned to the pre-training level by phlebotomy: VO₂peak −7 ± 7%, peak cardiac output −10 ± 7%. [Abs]
- Arbab-Zadeh 2014: 12 previously sedentary adults (7 men, 5 women, 29 ± 6 years) trained for 12 months [FT]; by the end 7 to 9 hours a week, long runs up to 3 hours, plus intervals [FT]; ten ran a marathon, one an Olympic-distance triathlon, one a 100-mile cycling race [FT]. VO₂max 40.3 to 48.7 mL/kg/min [Abs]. Left ventricular mass and wall thickness rose most in the first six months; left ventricular volume not significantly changed at three months and up only 8% at six months, with the largest increase in the second half of the year [FT]. Mass reached levels like elite athletes'; compliance approached but did not match them [Abs]. No control group [FT, Limitations]. MRI at baseline, 3, 6, 9, 12 months [Abs].
- 40.3 to 48.7 is +21% ("a fifth": my arithmetic). The paragraph on starting late (added 10 Oct after George's page comment) is my reading: the authors only raise growth as a possibility, and one year cannot separate "needs youth" from "needs longer". Grade C at best.
- Howden 2018 (same Dallas group; randomised controlled trial): 61 healthy sedentary adults (48% male, 53 ± 5 years), 34 assigned to two years of supervised training, 27 to control; 53 completed. VO₂max +18% in the training group (29.0 to 34.4 mL/kg/min) against 29.5 to 28.7 in controls. Left ventricular stiffness constant 0.072 to 0.051 with training, 0.0635 to 0.062 in controls. Measured by right heart catheterisation. [Abs] "Five decades" in the draft is from the mean age of 53; "did not compare with elite athletes" is from the abstract's silence and should be checked in the full text (free in PMC).
- Frank–Starling mechanism (a fuller heart ejects more per beat). [GK]

## Reading the research: Egan 2010 [all Abs]

- Eight sedentary men, two occasions, 400 kcal on a cycle ergometer at 40% or 80% of VO₂peak. Durations 69.9 and 36.0 minutes.
- PGC-1α mRNA at 3 h: 3.8-fold after the low-intensity ride, 10.2-fold after the high.
- AMPK phosphorylation 2.8-fold and CaMKII +84% immediately after the high-intensity ride, not after the low. p38 MAPK up after both.
- 10.2 / 3.8 = 2.7: my arithmetic. Not a claim the authors make about mitochondria.

## Detraining: Coyle 1984 [all Abs]

- Seven endurance-trained subjects, tested 12, 21, 56 and 84 days after stopping.
- VO₂max −7% in the first 21 days; stable after 56 days at 16% below the trained value.
- After 84 days still above eight sedentary controls who had never trained: 50.8 against 43.3 mL/kg/min.
- Stroke volume during exercise fell early, to a level not different from controls.
- Capillarisation did not decline; stayed 50% above controls.
- Citrate synthase and succinate dehydrogenase fell with a half-time of 12 days and settled 50% above controls.
- Early fall in VO₂max attributed to stroke volume, later fall to oxygen extraction.

## Individual differences

- HERITAGE (Bouchard 1999): 481 sedentary adults from 98 two-generation families, 20 weeks, VO₂max measured twice before and twice after. Mean gain about 400 mL/min; some little or no gain, others more than 1.0 L/min. 2.5 times more variance between families than within. Maximal heritability of the response 47%. All participants white ("Caucasian" in the paper). [Abs]
- Montero & Lundby 2017: 78 healthy adults in five groups doing one to five 60-minute sessions a week for six weeks. Non-response = change in maximal incremental power within the typical error of measurement, ±3.96%. Non-responders: 69%, 40%, 29%, 0%, 0%. Non-responders then did six more weeks with two extra sessions a week: none remained. Haemoglobin mass the strongest determinant of the VO₂max change. "One in five adults following physical activity guidelines are reported to not demonstrate any improvement." [Abs]
