## The question

4 × 4, 4 × 8 or 30/15: which interval session, and why?

## The argument (to approve)

1. **The position:** no single best session; in trained cyclists, sessions that pile up the most work close to the top of what can be sustained (4 to 8 minutes, or 30/15s) beat shorter-and-harder and longer-and-easier ones; long intervals beat sprints for time trials; sprints run all-out risk injury; order across weeks made no difference. Choose one or two formats, ride them at the hardest effort you can hold for the whole set, progress by adding work. Grade B.
2. What a session is made of: nine variables (Buchheit & Laursen 2013), plus a tenth, how hard it was ridden (effort-matched vs fixed).
3. **Close-up: Seiler 2013**, as a four-group table: 35 recreational cyclists on about six hours a week; 4 × 8 at about 90% of peak heart rate gained 11.4% against 5.5% (4 × 4), 5.6% (4 × 16) and 4.2% (easy only). Abstract only.
4. Long intervals: Helgerud 2007; Wisløff 2007 in heart-failure patients (side road); Lindsay 1996; Stepto 1999; Sylta 2016 (order irrelevant); Yang 2025's peak at about 140 s of work.
5. 30/15: Rønnestad 2015 and 2020, with the pacing caveat from Chapter 11 (Appelhans 2025). Grade B.
6. Threshold intervals: train the sustainable fraction and economy more than VO₂max (Seiler 2013's 4 × 16; Stöggl 2014; Tønnessen 2020; the Norwegian model); cheaper to recover from. My reading from Chapter 1's model; grade C.
7. Sprints: Gibala 2006; Gist 2014; Rosenblat 2020 (long HIIT 2% better for time trials); Helgerud 2023 (sprints raised anaerobic capacity, not VO₂max, and caused eight injuries in women running all-out). Grade B.
8. How hard: RPE about 17 (Seiler & Sjursen 2004); surges within intervals (Bossi 2020).
9. Fixed power or all-out: a smart trainer's fixed-power mode can cap a session (Appelhans 2025); set power from recent efforts. Grade C.
10. What each session does; recoveries (Seiler & Hetlelid 2005: about two minutes) and warm-up; a numbered list of five sessions; women and men; for six to ten hours.
